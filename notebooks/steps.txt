In this video, we will delve into the technical heart of the project, fine-tuning the M-BART model on a multi-lingual

corpus. The objective was simple to adapt the Facebook/M-BART large 50 model so that

it could effectively summarize text and English and French and a fluid, concise and relevant manner.

In step one, we install and import all the libraries needed for the project. Data sets for loading, manipulating,

and pre-processing NLP data sets. Transformers for accessing the Bart model in its tokenization tools.

Avaluate and RUJScore for automatically measuring the quality of the summaries produced. A found for

correcting encoding errors and raw texts. We also set up Google Drive via Colab. to permanently save

the train model, it's weights, results, and temporary files, ensuring an operational environment that is ready to

accommodate all phases of the project.

In step 2, we download two data sets specialized in automatic summarization, Edinburgh NLP/X

sum, a corpus of very short summaries extracted from BBC articles, a single sentence condensing the entire

article, and ML sum, a multilingual corpus of journalistic summaries from which we select the French

version, usually several sentences. Each data set structure differently hence the need to harmonize them in the following

steps.

In Step 3, we adapt the structure of the two data sets so that they share homogeneous columns.

We rename the main columns to unify them and add a Langdad column specifying the language of each example, which is crucial

information for guiding the multi-lingual tokenizer on-bar. This standardization ensures that

the two data sets are compatible for concatenation.

And step 4 we combine the English and French data into a single-balanced bilingual corpus. We can catenate all

the splits, train, validation, test, for each language. Apply random sampling limiting

each language to a maximum of 50,000-day examples. And then merge the two subsets into a final data set.

This choice allows us to control the size of the corpus for effective fine-tuning, while maintaining linguistic and thematic diversity.

Instead, we apply the FTFY.Fix Text function

to correct common encoding errors and data sets collected from the web. Invalid characters, accents, or misinterpreted

special symbols, we then calculate a badness-shoot score for each example to verify the readability

and cleanliness of the clean text.

In Step 6, we use regular expressions to remove any irrelevant elements. HTML tags,

email addresses, and URL links, multiple spaces, and unnecessary white space.

This operation aims to provide the model with input that is free of technical noise or web pollution.

In step 7, we harmonize the punctuation and type a graphical structure of the text. Removal of spaces

before punctuation marks, systematic addition of a space after each mark, and removal of repeated punctuation.

This formal consistency prevents subsequent tokenization errors.

In Step 8, we apply a series of filters to ensure the logical quality of the text in summary pairs.

Explicit conversion of all fields to character strings, removal of empty or corrupted examples, removal

of cases where the text in summary are identical. In exclusion of summaries that are longer than their source text,

non-compliant examples are removed from the final data set.

In step 9, we tokenize each example with the M-BART 50 tokenizer fast. Each text in

summary is converted into token identifiers, inputted. The language of the example is taken into

account via the Langtack field, and the sequences are truncated to 5-9-12 tokens for the text, and 128

for the summary. The data collator for SecU-2's Food and Dinner and Dree on David and Dirt the Intubious SecU

manages dynamic padding with an each batch.

In Step 10, we launch fine tuning of the Facebook/M Bart Large 50 model on our clean bilingual

data set using SecU2 SecU trainer, split 90% training get right 10% validation, several

epics with a valaus tracking. Early stopping a performance no longer improves, FE16 to speed up calculations

on compatible GPUs and gradient accumulation to simulate a larger batch size.

In step 11, we save the fine-tune model in its tokenizer with save-pre-trained in a Google drive folder,

which allows us to easily reload it via fromper-trained, deploy it in other applications without retraining and

share or archive the work done.

In step 12, we evaluate the quality of the generated summaries, quantitatively with Rouge metrics,

Rouge 1, Rouge 2, Rouge L on the validation set and qualitatively by generating summaries

on a subset of examples with controlled parameters, Numbemes, length penalty, temperature, etc.,

in order to judge the models' performance, both in terms of figures and concrete examples.

This comprehensive process enables us to obtain a Facebook/AVR at large 50 model, capable

of automatically summarizing texts in English and French with optimal performance. Are ready to use model for

automatic multi-lingual summarization?
